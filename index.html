AI as the intelligence layer, not a chatbot

AI is not a sixth world or an optional feature. It is the layer that understands, connects and serves all five worlds.

  • What it must understand: what a member is trying to do, which people, animals, plants, places, services or knowledge sources are relevant, what relationships connect them, what evidence supports an answer, and what action is permitted.
  • One identity: a member's people, animals, plants and places live under one account.
  • Privacy by design: the five worlds stay separate unless a member chooses to connect them. The rule is "isolate noise, connect meaning."
  • Knowing its limits: the AI must know when to answer, retrieve, ask a clarifying question, escalate to a person or abstain.

What the intelligence optimises for

Ekakul is designed to reward knowledge and positive value. It rejects engagement maximization as the objective.

  • A fixed objective: the founder sets what the system may optimise for. The AI decides how, and cannot redefine it.
  • Graded on value, not virality: public posts are to be assessed on quality, knowledge, truthfulness, benefit and non-harm. Criticism never lowers reach, and debate stays open.
  • Where grading never applies: Health content, minors and private posts.
  • Money cannot buy the algorithm: paid placement stays separate from organic ranking.
  • One karma: earned only by helping people, animals and plants, and confirmed by others, such as a vet or a blood bank. Posting, liking, following and giving money never earn it.
  • Still open: exact formulas, weights and thresholds are research questions, not settled policy.

Evidence, trust and misinformation

The design goes beyond a true-or-false classifier. It examines claims, sources, evidence, contradictions and uncertainty.

  • Evidence over assertion: claims are weighed against their sources, contradictions and uncertainty before any confidence is stated.
  • Authentic is not the same as true: a verified or signed source is not automatically correct.
  • Reports are not votes: a large number of reports never establishes falsehood by itself.
  • Fair to people: one false post does not make everything a creator says false. Assessment and enforcement are separate stages, with corrections and appeals.
  • No built-in conclusions: no subject is exempt from evidence, and disagreement is not proof of falsehood.

19 AI roles, each with a hard boundary

Nineteen AI roles are defined across the five worlds. Each is bound by its world’s rules.

  • Social: personal and community stewards, gathering and case agents, translation, voice notes and moderation triage. It proposes and never imposes, with no companion personas.
  • Animal: triage camera, rescue caseworker, passport steward, livestock advisor and identification. Never a diagnosis.
  • Plant: diagnosis camera, crop-calendar steward, voice-first answers and a mission verifier. No invented weather, disease certainty or pesticide advice.
  • Health: habit and family-calendar stewards, navigation and document organizing. Emergencies are routed to 108 or 112.
  • Entrepreneurship: scheme navigator, enterprise steward and skilling paths. It never applies on someone’s behalf and never gives financial advice.
  • Status: 6 roles have a detailed design and 13 are still to be designed.

Architecture and control

Models and providers are a replaceable layer behind Ekakul's own controls.

  • The model is never the authority: application services decide identity, permissions, transactions and any action that changes state. The model proposes; the application authorizes and executes.
  • Grounded answers: answers cite their sources.
  • Existing models first: replaceable models chosen on Ekakul’s own benchmarks. Fine-tuning comes only when evaluation shows a gap that prompts, retrieval and model choice cannot close. Pretraining a foundation model is outside the plan.
  • Data: member data is to be processed in Bharat, with no training on member content by default.

Evaluation, oversight and scale

No AI capability counts as ready because a demo looks convincing. Each needs a defined contract and a repeatable test suite.

  • Evaluation before release: suites in several languages gate every release. Safety-critical cases, such as routing emergencies to emergency numbers, must pass every time.
  • Human oversight: AI acts alone only on clear abuse such as spam, scams and explicit images. People decide everything else, with an appeal path and a kill switch.
  • Scale model: about 17 billion input tokens a month at one million members, mostly background work such as grading, moderation triage and embeddings. This is a planning estimate, not a measurement.
  • Roadmap with NVIDIA: three stages, retrieval and understanding, then fine-tuning, then controlled inference on GPUs hosted in Bharat. Each stage has an exit gate based on evaluation or measured volume.

Oversight and Control of AI Agents

The systems I use to build everything else. Independent work, built on my own time and equipment by directing AI coding agents, now mainly Claude Code. Figures come from system logs.

Runtime Gate for AI Coding Agents

A control layer for AI coding agents.

  • Across 86 agent sessions, it allowed 2,677 actions and refused 158.
  • The refusals included 12 attempts to skip a check, 6 attempts to change the gate itself and 11 deploys without authorization.
  • A companion commit gate checks every commit. In its first two days it ran on 127 real commits and blocked 22.

GV-PIT-DVM-I Lifecycle and PRANA

A nine-phase delivery lifecycle that I designed: Gather, Verify, Plan, Implement, Test, Deploy, Validate, Monitor, Iterate. Each phase ends in a gate.

  • PRANA is its fifth iteration and the engine behind the runtime and commit gates: 860 commits.
  • It governs multi-agent work at scale: up to 58 nested Claude Code sessions in one orchestration, with read-only rules and independent reviewer sessions.

Agent Failure Taxonomy

A written list of 15 recurring ways AI agents fail, each tied to a check that enforces against it.

  • Examples: certifying their own work, reporting work complete when it is not, and discarding prior work after feedback.
  • The taxonomy came from failures observed across my own agent-built projects, and it shapes what the gates check.

AI Engineering Operating System (AIEOS)

A single source of truth for the skills, rules and tools that my AI agents use, served to five agent runtimes: Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Hermes and OpenCode.

  • 1,027 commits and 72 skills. Of those, 32 form a full delivery lifecycle, each with a contract, a test and a table of the excuses agents use to skip steps.
  • "Done" is measured, never just claimed. In one snapshot, 10 of 106 standing instructions were verified in effect at runtime.

PKOS and ARI

Two supporting systems for knowledge and research.

  • PKOS: an engineering knowledge platform that runs without any AI model. It keeps evidence and decision records, with 416 checks across 15 suites passing.
  • ARI: a research agent that runs every four hours against explicit evidence criteria: 104 logged runs.
